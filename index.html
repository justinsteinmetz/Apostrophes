where they go, and where they don’t.

0 of 8 revealed

Why do you think so many people write apostrophes in plurals, like “apple’s”?

The rule that fixes most errors

An apostrophe never makes a plural.

Missing letters

don’t · it’s · they’re

The apostrophe stands in for letters that have been left out.

Belonging

Jess’s phone · the girls’ room

The apostrophe shows who or what something belongs to, or is connected to.

The “greengrocer’s apostrophe”: Apple’s £1. Wrong, and now you can’t unsee it.

Belonging

the three-step test

  1. Find the owner.
  2. Write the owner exactly as it is.
  3. Add ’s. If the owner is a plural already ending in s, add just ’.

Look-alikes

say the full words

With an apostrophe: short forNo apostrophe: belongs to
it’s = it is / it hasits = belonging to it
you’re = you areyour = belonging to you
they’re = they aretheir = belonging to them
who’s = who is / who haswhose = belonging to whom

Possessive pronouns never take an apostrophe: its, whose, yours, hers, ours, theirs.

Four special cases

Names ending in s

Jess’s phone · Jess’ phone

Both are correct. Pick one and stay consistent.

Decades and ages

the 1990s · in her 20s

Plural numbers: no apostrophe.

Abbreviations

CDs · MPs · PhDs

Plural abbreviations: no apostrophe.

Shortened years

the ’90s

Here the apostrophe stands for the missing 19.
